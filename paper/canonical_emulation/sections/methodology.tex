\section{Numerical methods and reproducibility}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Protected-memory construction}

The protected-memory QRC has two qubits, initial state $|00\rangle$, and
Hamiltonian
$H(u)=JX_1X_2+0.37(Z_1+Z_2)+0.64u(X_1+X_2)$.  Its collective jump is
$L_c=\sqrt{0.8}(\sigma_1^-+\sigma_2^-)$, and each step lasts
$\Delta t=0.2$.  An old flag applies either $I$ or $R_x^{(1)}(1)$ before the
symmetric channel.  The following 80 inputs have arbitrary $u\in[0,1]$ and no
local pulse.  Twelve independent roots sample $J\in[-1,1]$ and the suffix
stream.  We compare singlet population before and after the suffix,
check the analytic pulse population, and verify that symmetric preparation
does not populate the singlet sector.

The matched local-loss control replaces the collective jump by local
amplitude damping.  It reduces the median absolute singlet-population gap
after 80 steps to $1.569\times10^{-5}$.  A second control introduces relative
asymmetries $0.02$, $0.05$, $0.10$, and $0.20$ in the collective jump; each
produces the predicted nonzero initial singlet decay.  This breaks exact
conservation without implying rapid erasure at every tested asymmetry.

\subsection{Target QRCs and reset-QELM emulator}

The numerical target QRCs have $N_T\in\{2,3,4\}$ qubits and evolve as
\begin{align}
 H_T(u)&=\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}X_iX_j+0.5\sum_i Z_i+u\sum_iX_i,\nonumber\\
 \dot\rho&=-i[H_T(u),\rho]+\gamma_T\sum_i\mathcal D[\sigma_i^-]\rho,
 \label{eq:target-qrc-model}
\end{align}
where
$\mathcal D[L]\rho=L\rho L^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L^\dagger L,\rho\}$,
$J_{ij}\sim\mathcal U[-1,1]$, and $\Delta t=0.5$.  Inputs use a uniform
33-point alphabet on $[0,1]$.  The output contains every single-site Pauli
expectation and every same-axis two-site product, giving 9, 18, and 30
components for $N_T=2,3,4$, respectively.

The emulator is one fixed six-qubit parallel time-delayed QELM.  It resets
before each example and encodes the newest $d\leq6$ inputs on separate qubits,
using
$\sqrt{1-u}|0\rangle+\sqrt{u}|1\rangle$; unused qubits remain in
$|0\rangle$.  An independent random $XX$ Hamiltonian with
$J_{ij}\sim\mathcal U[-1,1]$ and transverse field $0.5$ evolves the product
state for $t_{\rm evol}=0.5$ without dissipation.  Single-site $X,Y,Z$
expectations are recorded at one, two, or four nested checkpoints, giving
feature budgets $B\in\{18,36,72\}$.  This fixed emulator is distinct from the
growing Bernstein family used as an explicit scalar-input witness.

Features are standardized using training rows only.  The affine ridge penalty
$\lambda$ is selected on validation data from
$\{0,10^{-10},10^{-8},10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10^2,10^4,10^6,\infty\}$
and refitted on the combined training and validation rows.  Finite penalties
use an economy singular-value decomposition with relative resolved-subspace
cutoff $10^{-6}$; $\lambda=\infty$ denotes the intercept-only model.

\subsection{Frozen cohorts and common-suffix panels}

The confirmatory cohort uses $N_T\in\{2,3\}$,
$\gamma_T\in\{0,0.2,0.5,1,2,4\}$, and $d\in\{1,2,4,6\}$.  The denser map adds
$d=3,5$ and loss rates $0.75,1.25,1.5,2.5,3$.  It uses 12 roots and 108 seeds
disjoint from the original experiment.  After that map was inspected, the
four-qubit follow-up was frozen prospectively with 12 new roots and 96
disjoint seeds.  It retains the same 11 loss rates, six windows, emulator,
feature budgets, sample sizes, and success rule.  Its registered checks test negative
history decay, a non-increasing minimum-window frontier, and the inherited
$\gamma_T=4,d=6,B=72$ anchor.  The three size cohorts are validated separately
and never pooled.

Within each root, train, validation, test, and suffix streams are independent.
Each streaming split begins in $|0\cdots0\rangle$ and has a 128-step washout,
followed by 480 training, 160 validation, and 320 test rows.  A separate panel
contains 32 length-six suffixes.  Four independently burned-in old prefixes
lead into every suffix; shorter windows are nested tails of the same sequence.
The newest suffix symbols are sampled without replacement from the 33-symbol
alphabet, while older coordinates are sampled independently.  Couplings,
input streams, and feature seeds are fixed within a root, and seed namespaces
are disjoint across roots and cohorts.

For suffix $s$, prefix branch $k$, target output $y_{sk}$, branch mean
$\bar y_s$, and one window-model prediction $p_s$,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:panel-decomposition} separates the realized squared error into
old-history variation and residual emulator error.  This held-out,
distribution-dependent diagnostic is not a direct estimator of $D_d$ or
$r_{\rm vis}$.

\subsection{Decision rules and controls}

Errors are divided by target variance before taking the square root.  Thus the
registered NRMSE tolerance $0.10$ means an RMSE equal to 10\% of the target
standard deviation.  A cell is history-sufficient when at least 10 of 12
roots have history NRMSE at or below this tolerance.  Conditional on 12
nonzero fair-sign contrasts, the one-sided probability of at least 10 agreeing
signs is $0.0193$.  Root blocks---not rows, observables, suffixes, or shot
replicates---are the independent statistical units.  Confidence intervals use
20,000 paired root-block bootstrap samples.

The registered decision order first excludes target variances below $10^{-8}$.
For the remaining cells it asks whether the sampled history floor exceeds the
tolerance; whether the total QELM error reaches the tolerance; and, if not,
whether the replay control reaches the tolerance.  This gives
history-limited, emulated, representation-limited, and unresolved outcomes
without treating feature count as a remedy for missing inputs.
Here, representation limited is an operational diagnosis: history passes,
the fitted QELM fails, and replay succeeds. It does not isolate the best
approximation attainable with the QELM features.

Linear delay, quadratic delay, dimension-matched random-tanh, intercept-only,
and reset--replay controls receive the same windows and data splits.  Replay
starts from one fixed state and applies the target QRC's final $d$ channels,
thereby removing the old prefix while retaining the dynamics and observables.
Retrained replay refits its output weights; compiled replay keeps a previously
trained affine readout.  The classical comparison selects the best of linear
delay, quadratic delay, and random-tanh separately in each cell.

The primary $0.10$, 10-of-12 frontier was fixed before the dense cohorts were
opened.  A post hoc sensitivity check reaggregates the same simulations at
tolerances $0.08$, $0.10$, and $0.12$ and success rules of 9, 10, or 11 roots.

\subsection{Finite-shot study}

The finite-shot study uses 12 four-qubit local-loss target QRCs at
$\gamma_T=4$ and $d=6$, with total per-input budgets
$Q\in\{300,1200,4800,19200\}$.  Each snapshot and global-Pauli setting
receives an equal integer share of $Q$; one joint bit-string multinomial draw
supplies all compatible single-site expectations.  Training, validation, and
test features are sampled independently in each of 16 replicates.  Within
each replicate, validation data select $\lambda$ for every $B$ and then select
$B$; test targets enter neither decision.  Every replicate repeats the full
fit-and-selection procedure before evaluation on independently sampled test
features.  Target QRC outputs remain exact expectation values.

\subsection{Larger targets and longer context}

A separate prospective study uses 12 new roots, five-qubit local-loss targets
with 45 outputs, $\gamma_T\in\{1,4\}$, and $d\in\{2,4,6,8,10\}$.
A ten-qubit reset-QELM supplies one qubit per input and remains fixed across
these windows; nested checkpoints give $B\in\{30,60,120\}$.
Splits, burn-in, controls, and fitting rules are unchanged; the diagnostic
uses 32 length-ten suffixes with four old prefixes each. Two duplicate
development runs and dense-backend equivalence tests precede the frozen
execution. All cells are retained. Paired history contrasts use 10,000
whole-root bootstrap draws; the QELM--quadratic comparison fixes $B=120$
before test evaluation. The larger register is an additional resource,
so cross-cohort differences cannot be attributed to window length alone.

\subsection{Scope and availability}

The confirmatory matrix is restricted to two- and three-qubit local-loss QRCs,
a 33-symbol input alphabet, exact expectation values, and one six-qubit
reset-QELM emulator.  The four-qubit phase map and finite-shot study are
separate 12-root extensions, as is the five-qubit long-context study.
The theorems concern fixed finite vectors of
observable expectations under the stated compactness, channel-continuity, and
free-suffix-concatenation assumptions.  They neither
make the tested feature map universal nor provide a sample-efficient training
algorithm.  All results are simulations; they exclude hardware, gate,
state-preparation-and-measurement, drift, and measurement-backaction errors.
