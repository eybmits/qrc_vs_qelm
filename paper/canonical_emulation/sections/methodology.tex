\section*{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection*{Teacher and student models}

The protected-memory teacher has two qubits, initial state $|00\rangle$, and
Hamiltonian
$H(u)=JXX+0.37(Z_1+Z_2)+0.64u(X_1+X_2)$.
The collective jump is
$L_c=\sqrt{0.8}(\sigma_1^-+\sigma_2^-)$ and each step lasts $dt=0.2$.
An old flag applies either $I$ or $R_x^{(1)}(1)$ before the symmetric channel;
the next 80 inputs have arbitrary $u\in[0,1]$ and no local pulse.  Twelve
independent roots sample $J\in[-1,1]$ and the suffix stream.  The checks compare
singlet population before and after the suffix, verify the analytic pulse
population, and confirm that symmetric preparation does not populate the
singlet sector.  Matched local loss and asymmetric collective jumps remove the
protection while leaving the target observable unchanged.

The confirmatory finite-resource teachers have $N_T\in\{2,3\}$ qubits and obey
\begin{align}
 H_T(u)&=\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}X_iX_j+0.5\sum_i Z_i+u\sum_iX_i,\nonumber\\
 \dot\rho&=-i[H_T(u),\rho]+\gamma_T\sum_i\mathcal D[\sigma_i^-]\rho,
 \label{eq:teacher-model}
\end{align}
Here
$\mathcal D[L]\rho=L\rho L^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L^\dagger L,\rho\}$,
$J_{ij}\sim\mathcal U[-1,1]$, $dt=0.5$, and
$\gamma_T\in\{0,0.2,0.5,1,2,4\}$.  Inputs are uniform on 33 equally spaced
values in $[0,1]$.  The output contains every single-site Pauli expectation
and every same-axis two-site product, giving 9 components for $N_T=2$ and 18
for $N_T=3$.

The constructive theorem uses a growing Bernstein QELM.  Each of the $d$
scalar lag coordinates is encoded into $r$ freshly prepared qubits, all qubits
are measured once in the computational basis, and outcomes are coarse-grained
to the tuple of blockwise Hamming weights.  A coefficient lookup followed by
averaging is the linear readout.  This family is used for the existence and
resource bound; its full coefficient table is not fitted in the numerical
phase map.

The numerical student is instead one fixed six-qubit parallel time-delayed
QELM.  It is reset before each example and receives the newest
$d\in\{1,2,4,6\}$ inputs, one per qubit.
Input $u$ is encoded as
$\sqrt{1-u}|0\rangle+\sqrt{u}|1\rangle$ on its assigned qubit; unused qubits
remain in $|0\rangle$.  An independent random $XX$ Hamiltonian with
$J_{ij}\sim\mathcal U[-1,1]$ and transverse field $0.5$ evolves the product
state for $T=0.5$ without dissipation.  Single-site $X,Y,Z$ expectations are
recorded at $T/4,T/2,3T/4,T$.  The nested feature sets use $\{T\}$,
$\{T/2,T\}$, or all four checkpoints, giving $B\in\{18,36,72\}$.

Features are standardized from training rows only.  The affine ridge penalty
is selected from
$\{0,10^{-10},10^{-8},10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10^2,10^4,10^6,\infty\}$
on validation data and refitted on the combined training and validation rows.
Finite penalties use an economy SVD with relative resolved-subspace cutoff
$10^{-6}$; $\alpha=\infty$ is the intercept-only model.

The constructive audit uses the existing one-qubit
\texttt{LindbladReservoir} with $H(u)=0.5Z+uX$, local loss $\gamma=4$, and
$dt=0.5$.  Block-Hamming probabilities are checked directly on a six-qubit
statevector at $d=2,r=3$.  Bernstein errors use a $41\times41$ closed input
grid and $r\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$.  History truncation is evaluated on one
fixed 257-input continuous stream for $d=1,\ldots,8$.  The runner writes
deterministic CSV and JSON artifacts with a SHA-256 ledger.

Quantum circuit probabilities agree with the Bernstein features to
$8.33\times10^{-17}$.  On the two-input replay map, increasing $r=1\to32$
reduces the maximum representation error from $0.043774$ to $0.0014373$.
On the fixed stream, increasing $d=1\to8$ reduces the maximum
recurrent-versus-replay error from $0.099479$ to $3.83\times10^{-5}$.  Every
observed error lies below its analytic bound.

\subsection*{Common-suffix measurement}

Each of the 12 root blocks fixes teacher couplings, student couplings, input
streams, and random-feature seeds; seed namespaces are disjoint across roots.
Train, validation, test, and suffix streams are independent.  Each streaming
split begins in $|0\cdots0\rangle$ and has a 128-step washout followed by 480
training, 160 validation, and 320 test rows.  A separate panel contains 32
length-six suffixes.  Four independent old prefixes, each after a 128-step
burn-in, lead into every suffix; shorter windows are nested tails of the same
sequence.  The newest suffix symbols are sampled without replacement from the
33-symbol alphabet, and older coordinates are sampled independently.

For suffix $s$, prefix branch $k$, teacher output $y_{sk}$, branch mean
$\bar y_s$, and QELM prediction $p_s$, the panel separates
\begin{align}
 R_{\rm hist}&=m^{-1}\mathbb E_{s,k}\|y_{sk}-\bar y_s\|^2,\nonumber\\
 R_{\rm rep}&=m^{-1}\mathbb E_s\|\bar y_s-p_s\|^2,\nonumber\\
 R_{\rm total}&=R_{\rm hist}+R_{\rm rep}.
 \label{eq:panel-decomposition}
\end{align}
The first term is output variation among histories that the student cannot
distinguish.  The second is the residual once the suffix is known.  The
identity is exact on every realized panel.

\subsection*{Evaluation, controls, and decision rules}

Errors are normalized by the target variance before taking the square root.
The feasibility threshold is NRMSE $0.10$.  A target with variance below
$10^{-8}$ is a degenerate target (D).  The remaining cells are classified in
order as history impossible (H) when the common-suffix floor exceeds the
threshold, emulated (E) when total QELM error is below it, representation
limited (R) when the suffix is sufficient but the QELM remains above the
threshold, and unresolved (U) otherwise.  A tie among root-modal categories is
shown as mix.  Root blocks, not rows, observables, or shot replicates, are the
independent statistical units.  Intervals use 20,000 root-block bootstrap
replicates.

Quadratic delay, linear delay, dimension-matched random-tanh, intercept-only,
and reset--replay controls receive the same windows and data splits.  Replay
starts from one fixed state $\sigma$ and applies the teacher's last $d$
channels.  It therefore removes the old prefix while preserving the dynamics
and observables.  Retrained replay refits its output weights, whereas compiled
replay keeps a previously trained affine readout.  The parallel QELM is a
different model: it encodes the entire window at once.  The classical
comparison selects the best of quadratic delay, linear delay, and random-tanh
in each cell.

Seven registered checks cover artifact integrity, the exact panel
decomposition, agreement with independent reset replay, observation of both
history-feasible and history-limited regimes, at least $20\%$ window and
feature gains in at least 10/12 roots, and replication across both teacher
sizes.  All seven pass.  A separate quantum-specific check requires at least a
$10\%$ median gain over the best classical control and a positive gain in at
least 10/12 roots; it fails.

The dissipation--window analysis fits
$\log h(d)=a-c\gamma_Tdt\,d$ to the 20 positive-loss cells for each root and
teacher size, where $h(d)$ is the common-suffix history NRMSE.

\subsection*{Dissipator, observable, and finite-shot studies}

The observable-memory study uses 12 independent roots,
$N_T\in\{2,3,4\}$, $d\in\{1,2,4,8\}$, nominal strengths
$\gamma\in\{0.25,1,4\}$, four prefix branches, a 48-step prefix burn-in, and a
13-symbol input alphabet with 12 suffix groups.  It compares local loss, collective loss, and
dephasing on three output panels: single- and two-site same-axis $XYZ$,
single-site $XY$, and single-site $Z$.  Jump families are normalized to the
Hilbert--Schmidt jump strength of local loss at the same nominal $\gamma$;
this matching does not imply equal Liouvillian gaps.  A paired variant draws
continuous inputs from $\mathcal U[0,1]$.

The finite-shot study in Fig.~\ref{fig:resource-diagnosis}\,c uses 12 roots of four-qubit local-loss
teachers at $\gamma_T=4$, $d=6$, and total per-input budgets
$Q\in\{300,1200,4800,19200\}$.  Each snapshot/global-Pauli setting receives an
equal integer share of $Q$; one joint bit-string multinomial outcome supplies
all compatible single-site expectations.  Training, validation, and test
features are sampled independently in each of 16 replicates.  Within each
replicate, validation data select $\alpha$ separately for every $B$ and then
select $B$; test targets enter neither choice.  Teacher labels remain exact.
Because feature noise in the training and validation splits changes both the
fitted readout and the selected budget, every replicate repeats the complete
fit-and-selection procedure before evaluation on its independently sampled
test features.

\subsection*{Finite-panel calibration}

The four prefix branches make Eq.~\eqref{eq:panel-decomposition} exact for each
realized suffix panel, and the classifications use this empirical floor.  If
the branches are conditionally independent samples from a population of old
histories, the empirical history numerator has expectation $(K-1)/K$ times the
population conditional variance.  Its unbiased correction is therefore $4/3$
at $K=4$.  Repeating the classification with this correction changes 39 of
1,728 root-level labels from unresolved to history impossible and four of 144
modal map cells; no emulated or representation-limited label changes.  The
reported window and feature effect sizes remain unchanged.

\subsection*{Scope and reproducibility}

The confirmatory matrix is restricted to two- and three-qubit local-loss
teachers, a 33-symbol input alphabet, exact expectation values, and one fixed
six-qubit QELM architecture.  The four-qubit, alternative-dissipator,
continuous-input, and finite-shot studies are separate 12-root numerical
extensions.  The growing Bernstein-QELM theorem concerns bounded observable
outputs under its stated continuity assumptions; it does not grant universality
to the tested six-qubit local-Pauli feature map or provide a sample-efficient
training algorithm.  All numerical results are simulations; they do not
include hardware, gate or state-preparation-and-measurement errors,
measurement backaction, or drift.  The target is the declared bounded
observable output rather than the complete QRC state.

Source code, configurations, raw result tables, figure sources, and the
content-addressed release manifest are available at
\href{https://github.com/eybmits/qrc_vs_qelm}{github.com/eybmits/qrc\_vs\_qelm}.
