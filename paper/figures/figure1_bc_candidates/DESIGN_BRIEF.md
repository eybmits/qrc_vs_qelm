## Panel b: missing history

The recent input window is identical, while earlier inputs may differ. A
window-only predictor must give one prediction. The QRC's compatible outputs
can differ. With increasing context, the full compatible-output diameter can
vanish, or retain a positive floor. For scalar output, the optimal unrestricted
worst-case history error equals half the largest compatible-output gap. Do not
call a fixed tested QELM universal or infer a worst-case statement from an
arbitrarily chosen pair of sample trajectories. Envelopes denote admissible
output ranges, never confidence intervals or empirical data.

Five distinct visual explanations are wanted, not recolorings of one curve:
consider a compatible-output funnel, a same-window collision, an interval
ladder, a tolerance crossing, and repeated quantum channels carrying or
forgetting an old influence. A candidate may foreground one aspect, and its
caption must state exactly what it does and does not illustrate.

## Panel c: output-relevant versus permanently invisible differences

Start with visibly different input-created states, not merely two unlabeled
output lines. Under the same later inputs, some state differences can affect
an output now or later; others never affect any declared output under any
allowed continuation. The latter need not fade. Being invisible at the current
time alone is insufficient. A drawing cannot establish the universal
quantifier: the permanently invisible case is an explicitly stipulated
condition, not an empirical inference from finitely many overlapping curves.

Five genuinely different explanations are wanted: consider paired-state
pipelines, a connection/path to observed nodes, now-versus-later measurement,
an invariant invisible direction, and grouping states by identical future
output behavior. Avoid implying that every persistent quantum state distinction
obstructs finite-window emulation. These panels classify relevant information;
they do not alone establish fading, efficient learning or quantum advantage.
