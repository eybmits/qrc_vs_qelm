\subsection{Numerical Methods and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsubsection{Protected-Memory Construction}

The two-qubit protected-memory QRC starts in $|00\rangle$ with Hamiltonian
$H(u)=JX_1X_2+0.37(Z_1+Z_2)+0.64u(X_1+X_2)$.  Its collective jump is
$L_c=\sqrt{0.8}(\sigma_1^-+\sigma_2^-)$, and each step lasts
$\Delta t=0.2$.  An old flag applies $I$ or $R_x^{(1)}(1)$ before the
symmetric channel, followed by 80 inputs $u\in[0,1]$ without local pulses.
Twelve independent roots sample $J\in[-1,1]$ and suffixes.  Checks compare
singlet populations before and after each suffix, verify the analytic pulse
population, and confirm that symmetric preparation leaves the singlet empty.

Replacing the collective jump with matched local amplitude damping reduces
the median absolute gap after 80 steps to $1.569\times10^{-5}$.  Relative
collective-jump asymmetries $0.02$, $0.05$, $0.10$, and $0.20$ each produce
the predicted nonzero initial singlet decay: exact conservation is broken,
without necessarily causing rapid erasure.

\subsubsection{Target QRCs and Reset-QELM Emulator}

The numerical target QRCs have $N_T\in\{2,3,4\}$ qubits and evolve as
\begin{align}
 H_T(u)&=\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}X_iX_j+0.5\sum_i Z_i+u\sum_iX_i,\nonumber\\
 \dot\rho&=-i[H_T(u),\rho]+\gamma_T\sum_i\mathcal D[\sigma_i^-]\rho,
 \label{eq:target-qrc-model}
\end{align}
where
$\mathcal D[L]\rho=L\rho L^\dagger-\tfrac12\{L^\dagger L,\rho\}$,
$J_{ij}\sim\mathcal U[-1,1]$, and $\Delta t=0.5$.  Inputs use a uniform
33-point alphabet on $[0,1]$.  All single-site Pauli expectations and
same-axis two-site products give 9, 18, and 30 outputs for $N_T=2,3,4$.

The fixed six-qubit time-delayed QELM resets each example and encodes the
newest $d\leq6$ inputs on separate qubits as
$\sqrt{1-u}|0\rangle+\sqrt{u}|1\rangle$; unused qubits remain in
$|0\rangle$.  An independent $XX$ Hamiltonian with
$J_{ij}\sim\mathcal U[-1,1]$ and transverse field $0.5$ evolves the product
state for $t_{\rm evol}=0.5$ without dissipation.  Single-site $X,Y,Z$
expectations at one, two, or four nested checkpoints give
$B\in\{18,36,72\}$.  This emulator differs from the growing Bernstein witness.

Training rows alone determine feature standardization.  Validation selects
the affine ridge penalty $\lambda$ from
$\{0,10^{-10},10^{-8},10^{-6},10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10^2,10^4,10^6,\infty\}$
before refitting on training and validation rows.  Finite penalties use an
economy singular-value decomposition with relative resolved-subspace cutoff
$10^{-6}$; $\lambda=\infty$ is the intercept-only model.

\subsubsection{Frozen Cohorts and Common-Suffix Panels}

The confirmatory cohort uses $N_T\in\{2,3\}$,
$\gamma_T\in\{0,0.2,0.5,1,2,4\}$, and $d\in\{1,2,4,6\}$.  The denser map adds
$d=3,5$ and loss rates $0.75,1.25,1.5,2.5,3$, using 12 roots and 108 disjoint
seeds.  After inspecting that map, the four-qubit follow-up was frozen
prospectively with 12 new roots and 96 disjoint seeds.  It retains all 11
rates, six windows, emulator, budgets, sample sizes, and success rule.
Registered checks test negative history decay, a non-increasing minimum-window
frontier, and the inherited $\gamma_T=4,d=6,B=72$ anchor.  Size cohorts are
validated separately, never pooled.

Within each root, train, validation, test, and suffix streams are independent.
Streaming splits start in $|0\cdots0\rangle$ with 128 washout steps, then
480 training, 160 validation, or 320 test rows.  The separate panel has 32
length-six suffixes, each following four independently burned-in prefixes;
shorter windows are nested tails.  Newest suffix symbols are sampled without
replacement from the 33-symbol alphabet; older coordinates are independent.
Couplings, streams, and feature seeds are fixed within roots; seed namespaces
are disjoint across roots and cohorts.

Equation~\eqref{eq:panel-decomposition} separates realized squared error into
old-history variation and residual emulator error.  This held-out,
distribution-dependent diagnostic does not directly estimate $D_d$ or $r_{\rm vis}$.

\subsubsection{Decision Rules and Controls}

MSE is divided by target variance before taking its square root.  The
registered $0.10$ NRMSE tolerance therefore means 10\% of the target standard
deviation; history sufficiency requires at least 10 of 12 roots below or at
this tolerance.  For 12 nonzero fair-sign contrasts, at least 10 agreeing
signs has one-sided probability $0.0193$.  Independent units are root blocks,
not rows, observables, suffixes, or shot replicates.  Confidence intervals
use 20,000 paired root-block bootstraps.

The registered decision order excludes target variances below $10^{-8}$,
then checks history-floor failure, total-QELM success, and replay
success.  Outcomes are history limited, emulated, representation
limited, or unresolved; additional features do not remedy missing inputs.
Here, representation limited is an operational diagnosis: history passes,
the fitted QELM fails, and replay succeeds. It does not isolate the best
approximation attainable with the QELM features.

Linear delay, quadratic delay, dimension-matched random-tanh, intercept-only,
and reset--replay controls share windows and data splits.  Replay applies the
target's final $d$ channels to one fixed state, retaining dynamics and
observables but removing the old prefix.  Retrained replay refits weights;
compiled replay retains a trained affine readout.  The best linear delay,
quadratic delay, or random-tanh control is selected separately per cell.

The $0.10$, 10-of-12 frontier was fixed before opening dense cohorts.
Post hoc sensitivity checks use tolerances $0.08$, $0.10$, and $0.12$ and
9, 10, or 11 required roots on the same simulations.

\subsubsection{Finite-Shot Study}

Twelve four-qubit local-loss targets use $\gamma_T=4$, $d=6$, and per-input
budgets $Q\in\{300,1200,4800,19200\}$.  Each snapshot and global-Pauli setting
receives an equal integer share; one joint multinomial bit-string draw
supplies compatible single-site expectations.  All 16 replicates independently
sample training, validation, and test features and repeat complete fitting
and selection.  Validation selects $\lambda$ for each $B$, then $B$ itself;
test targets enter neither decision.  Target outputs remain exact expectations.

\subsubsection{Larger Targets and Longer Context}

The prospective extension uses 12 new roots, five-qubit local-loss targets,
45 outputs, $\gamma_T\in\{1,4\}$, and $d\in\{2,4,6,8,10\}$.
A fixed ten-qubit reset-QELM encodes one input per qubit with
$B\in\{30,60,120\}$. Other settings are inherited, except the 32 diagnostic
suffixes now have length ten. Dense-equivalence tests and duplicate smokes
precede freezing. All cells remain; contrasts use 10,000 root bootstrap
draws and the classical comparison fixes $B=120$. Cross-cohort changes
include the larger register, not context alone.

\subsubsection{Scope and Availability}

The confirmatory, dense-map, long-context, and finite-shot cohorts remain
separate. The theorems' assumptions do not imply universal tested features
or sample-efficient training. All evidence is simulated, excluding hardware,
gate, state-preparation-and-measurement, drift, and backaction errors.

Code, protocols, and reproducibility details are collected in the companion
repository; its identifying link is withheld for double-blind review.
